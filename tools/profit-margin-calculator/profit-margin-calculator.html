Understanding Margin vs Markup

Although business owners often use the terms Margin and Markup interchangeably, they represent two different pricing metrics. Knowing the mathematical difference between them is vital for setting prices that ensure profitability:

  • Profit Margin: Represents profit as a percentage of the selling price. It is computed as:
    Margin (%) = ((Selling Price - Cost) / Selling Price) * 100
  • Markup: Represents the ratio of profit to the cost price (how much the price is increased to reach selling value). It is computed as:
    Markup (%) = ((Selling Price - Cost) / Cost) * 100

Why the Difference Matters:

If you purchase an item for $100 and want to achieve a **30% Profit Margin**, setting a 30% markup is a common error. A 30% markup sets your selling price to $130, which actually yields a gross profit margin of only **23.1%** ($30 profit divided by $130 revenue). To target a true 30% margin, your selling price must be **$142.86** ($100 Cost / (1 - 0.30)).
