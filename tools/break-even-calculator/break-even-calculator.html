How to Calculate Your Business Break-Even Point

The Break-Even Point (BEP) is the sales milestone where a business's total revenue equals its total expenses. At this point, the business makes zero profit and incurs zero loss. Computing the break-even threshold is a critical step in business planning to determine if a pricing strategy or product venture is viable.

The Formulas:

  1. Contribution Margin per Unit: The amount of money each unit sold contributes towards covering fixed overhead costs.
    Contribution Margin = Selling Price - Variable Cost
  2. Break-Even Units: The number of items you must sell to break even.
    Break-Even Units = Total Fixed Costs / Contribution Margin per Unit
  3. Break-Even Sales ($): The gross revenue amount you must earn to break even.
    Break-Even Sales = Break-Even Units * Unit Selling Price

Fixed vs Variable Costs:

  • Fixed Costs: Continuous business expenses that do not change regardless of how many units you manufacture or sell. Examples: warehouse rent, base office salaries, business insurance, and equipment leasing.
  • Variable Costs: Transactional expenses that scale directly with production volumes. Examples: product materials, packaging shipping labels, credit card processor fees, and direct manufacturing labor wages.
