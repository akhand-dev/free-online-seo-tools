Guide to United Kingdom VAT (Value Added Tax)

Value Added Tax (VAT) is a consumption tax charged on the sale of goods and services in the UK. Businesses with taxable turnovers exceeding the registration limit (currently £90,000 as of 2024/2026) must register for VAT with HMRC and file regular tax returns under the **Making Tax Digital (MTD)** framework.

Standard UK VAT Rates:

  • Standard Rate (20%): Applies to most general goods, services, electronics, clothes, and standard business invoices.
  • Reduced Rate (5%): Charged on specific services, including domestic utilities/heating fuel, child car seats, and specific residential renovations.
  • Zero Rate (0%): Covers essential items. No tax is added, but transactions must be reported. Examples: basic foods, books/newspapers, children's clothes, and public transport.

Mathematical Formulas:

  1. Adding VAT (Exclusive to Inclusive):
    Gross = Net * (1 + VAT Rate / 100)
  2. Removing VAT (Inclusive to Exclusive):
    Net = Gross / (1 + VAT Rate / 100)
