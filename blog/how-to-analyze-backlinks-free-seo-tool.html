How to Check My Backlinks for Free & Perform Backlink Analysis

A backlink represents a critical vote of confidence in Google's search algorithms. In this complete guide, you will learn how to analyze backlinks for free, audit your backlink profile, inspect anchor text ratios, and verify dofollow link juice.

🛠 FREE TOOL: You can audit and analyze any referring page right now using our Free Backlink Analyzer & Checker.

Why Backlink Analysis is Critical for Search Rankings

Google's original PageRank algorithm was founded on the principle that academic papers with more citations are more authoritative. On the web, citations are hyperlinks. When authoritative websites link to your content, search crawlers perceive your domain as trustworthy and relevant.

However, performing routine backlink analysis is essential for three major reasons:

  1. Verifying Inbound Link Indexing: Confirm that guest posts, editorial mentions, and PR citations have not been deleted or marked as noindex.
  2. Auditing Dofollow vs Nofollow Juice: Make sure links meant to boost rankings are not accidentally marked with rel="nofollow", rel="sponsored", or rel="ugc".
  3. Protecting Against Over-Optimization: Ensure your anchor texts are diverse and not triggering Google spam filters.

Step-by-Step: How to Check Inbound Backlinks for Free

Method 1: Inspect Page Source Markup with our Analyzer

Whenever you acquire a link on a partner blog, directory, or media site, open that webpage, right-click, and select View Page Source. Paste the markup into our Backlink Analyzer. The tool will parse every <a> tag and highlight:

  • Whether the link points to your exact target domain
  • The visible anchor text string
  • Whether the link is Dofollow, Nofollow, UGC, or Sponsored

Method 2: Google Search Console (GSC) Links Report

Google provides free link data directly in your Search Console dashboard:

  1. Log into Google Search Console.
  2. Scroll down to the Links tab on the left sidebar.
  3. Under External Links, explore:
    • Top linked pages: Which of your URLs have earned the most backlinks.
    • Top linking sites: The root domains sending you the most links.
    • Top linking text: The actual anchor texts Googlebot has crawled.

Understanding Backlink Anchor Text Health

The anchor text used in a hyperlink signals what the destination page is about. An unnatural backlink profile dominated by 100% exact-match keywords (e.g., "buy cheap shoes online") is a red flag for Google Penguin algorithms.

A healthy, organic anchor text breakdown should follow this distribution:

  • Branded Anchors (40–50%): Your site name or brand (e.g. RankifyTools).
  • Naked URL Anchors (20–25%): Direct web address (e.g. free-online-seo-tools.vercel.app).
  • Partial Match / Topical (15–20%): Descriptive variations (e.g. check keyword density with RankifyTools).
  • Exact Match (Under 5%): Exact competitive keyword (e.g. analyze backlinks).
  • Generic Anchors (5–10%): Standard conversational phrases (e.g. click here, read more).
